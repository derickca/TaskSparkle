Signing in

How an account starts, and how you get back in.

Your account

The first account is made on the setup page a new copy shows while nobody has one, and that person is the administrator. On a copy hosted for other people, the setup page also asks for the one-time setup code, so a stranger who finds a brand-new copy cannot take the first account. Everyone after the first arrives by invite: an administrator sends a link from Settings, then Admin, and following it makes the account. An invite works once and lasts seven days, and an expired invite is replaced by a fresh one.

The ways in

Each person signs in with their own name — with a passkey, with a password, or with a passkey alone. The two ways stand equal, and an account can hold either or both; an account that starts with a passkey holds no password at all. Signing in is passkey-first: at a secure address the page opens on the passkey, a small switch below it — "Use a password instead" — trades to the password form, and the device remembers which view you last used. You can add a passkey on the page that makes your account, or later in Settings, then Account.

A secure address

Passkeys need a secure address: HTTPS, or a localhost address. At an ordinary HTTP address there are no passkey acts and no switch: the password form stands alone, and the page says the site is not encrypted. Passkeys already made still list on the Account page at such an address, and can be removed there.

Getting back in

Forgot the password, or lost every passkey? The administrator sends a fresh link from Settings, then Admin. Spending it sets a new password or enrols a new passkey, and signs out every other sign-in. A reset never removes a passkey on its own: a passkey comes off only by a named act — removing it yourself in Account, an administrator removing it, or the start-fresh choice on the link itself.
